Training holds sixteen bytes per parameter, so a 7B model does not fit on an 80 GB card. Six ways to cut it up, and what each one costs on the wire.

Budget 16Ψ — derived, not quoted Grid tp=4 × pp=2 × dp=4 = 32 GPUs Hard constraint TP inside one NVLink domain

Inference holds weights and a KV cache. Training holds four things, three of them invisible on a model card — and once you can total them, every parallelism strategy reads as an answer to the same arithmetic problem.

Part onethe budget

Sixteen bytes per parameter, derived

Walk one mixed-precision Adam step and each term is forced on you:

  1. bf16 parameters — 2 B/param

    Forward and backward run in 16-bit; that is the whole point of mixed precision, since tensor cores are 2× faster at 16-bit.

  2. Gradients — 2 B/param (or 4)

    PyTorch DDP accumulates in the parameter dtype. Megatron-LM accumulates into an fp32 main-gradient buffer, because summing thousands of small bf16 gradients loses bits to rounding.

  3. fp32 master parameters — 4 B/param

    The optimizer step cannot run in 16-bit. At step 100,000 a typical update changes a weight by a relative ~1e-7; bf16 has ~3 decimal digits of mantissa, so w + Δ rounds straight back to w and training silently stops learning.

  4. Adam moments m and v — 4 + 4 B/param

    Exponential moving averages of the gradient and the squared gradient, both fp32.

Read those three numbers and the whole field makes sense

A 7B model does not fit on an 80 GB GPU for training, even though it fits four times over for inference. Everything that follows is a scheme for cutting 16Ψ down or splitting the compute — and every scheme buys memory with network traffic.

The fourth consumer is activations — intermediates saved during forward because backward needs them. Unlike the first three, activation memory does not scale with parameter count. It scales with batch size, sequence length and depth, which is why a job can pass its startup allocation and then OOM at step 3 on its first long sequence.

The axes are orthogonal — apply all of them at once and the degrees multiply to the world size. tp=4, pp=2, dp=4 is 32 GPUs.

The one row that constrains your hardware

TP must stay inside one NVLink domain. It all-reduces roughly twice per transformer block in forward and twice in backward — that is dozens of collectives per step, each on the critical path. At 450 GB/s over NVLink that is tolerable; over a 50 GB/s NIC it is not.

This is the direct link back to lesson 02b.4: tp_size ≤ 8 on an HGX baseboard is not a convention, it is the width of the non-blocking domain.

Part threechoosing

Which axis, in what order

  1. Fill the NVLink domain with TP first

    It has the heaviest per-step traffic and the tightest latency requirement, so give it the fastest link you own. Then stop at the domain boundary.

  2. Use PP to cross node boundaries

    Point-to-point activation sends at stage boundaries only — the cheapest inter-node traffic of any axis. The cost is the pipeline bubble, not bandwidth.

  3. Use ZeRO/FSDP to shard what is left

    ZeRO-1 is nearly free (12Ψ saved, one reduce-scatter replacing one all-reduce). ZeRO-3 saves the most and costs the most: an all-gather per unit, in both passes.

  4. Use DP for throughput once it fits

    One all-reduce per step over the fabric. This is the axis you scale for speed, after the others have made it fit.

  5. Reach for CP only when sequence length is the problem

    It splits activations, not weights — so it does nothing for a model-state OOM and everything for a long-context one.

Using itdrills

Drill yourself

Why does a 7B model fit for inference but not for training on an 80 GB GPU?

Inference holds bf16 weights: 14 GB. Training holds 16 bytes per parameter — bf16 params, gradients, an fp32 master copy, and Adam's two fp32 moments — which is 112 GB, before a single activation.

Why can't the optimizer step run in bf16?

At step 100,000 a typical update changes a weight by a relative amount of order 1e-7. bf16 carries roughly 3 decimal digits of mantissa, so w + Δ rounds straight back to w.

Training does not crash — it silently stops learning, which is far worse. Hence the fp32 master copy.

Why must tensor parallelism stay inside one node?

It all-reduces about twice per transformer block in forward and twice in backward — dozens of collectives per step, every one on the critical path. That is tolerable at NVLink's 450 GB/s per direction and ruinous over a 50 GB/s NIC.

tp_size ≤ 8 is the width of the NVSwitch non-blocking domain, not a convention.

You OOM at step 3, having started fine. Which term grew?

Activations. Model state (16Ψ) is fixed the moment Ψ is fixed and is allocated up front. Activations scale with batch size, sequence length and depth — so the first long sequence in your data is what tips it.

Fixes are activation checkpointing, or context/sequence parallelism. Sharding the optimizer will not help.

ZeRO-1 vs ZeRO-3: what does each buy and cost?

ZeRO-1 shards optimizer states only — saves 12Ψ·(1−1/N), and replaces one all-reduce with a reduce-scatter plus all-gather. Nearly free.

ZeRO-3 additionally shards gradients and parameters — saves the full 16Ψ·(1−1/N), but needs roughly two all-gathers and a reduce-scatter per unit, so the traffic scales with depth rather than being once per step.

Which axis uses all-to-all, and why is that different?

Expert parallelism, twice per MoE layer — route tokens to their expert, route results back. It is not an all-reduce, so it has a different congestion profile: every rank talks to every other with a distinct payload rather than a symmetric reduction.

That makes it far more sensitive to fabric imbalance than an all-reduce is.
