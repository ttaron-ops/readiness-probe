The metric that
measures waiting,
not busy-ness

Load average counts waiters whether or not anyone needs them. PSI counts only work that was denied a resource — and the difference between its two lines is the difference between contention and lost throughput.

Answers the saturation question load average cannot Centrepiece one cgroup, three tasks, 200 ms Rule busy is not starved

Lesson 01b.1 ended by saying load average is not a saturation metric and pointing here. This is the instrument that is — and its whole design is one idea: measure the time work spent waiting, not the time a resource spent busy.

The distinction in one line each

some — at least one task was stalled. Something is being delayed.

full — every runnable task was stalled. Nothing productive happened at all. This is the number that converts directly into lost throughput.

Sleeping tasks are invisible, and that is the whole trick

A process blocked in epoll_wait because there is no work is not stalled — it is idle. PSI counts only tasks that want a resource and cannot have it.

Compare with load average from lesson 01b.1, which counts nr_uninterruptible regardless of whether anyone is waiting on the result. That is exactly why load says 80 on a hung mount and PSI says what fraction of real work was lost.

Part twothe files

Reading the four lines correctly

FieldUnitExactly what it is
avg10percent of wall timeEWMA of the 2-second samples with decay e^(−2/10) per period — a 10-second time constant, not a window
avg60 / avg300percentsame, with decay e^(−2/60) and e^(−2/300)
totalmicroseconds, monotoniccumulative non-idle-weighted stall time since boot, or since cgroup creation. The kernel accumulates in ns and divides by 1000 on output.
Use total, not the averages, for anything you alert on

The three averages are decayed EWMAs — same structural lag as load average, for the same reason. total is a monotonic microsecond counter, so a rate over your own window is both exact and free of the kernel's choice of time constant.

/proc/pressure/irq exists only with CONFIG_IRQ_TIME_ACCOUNTING, and only has a full line.

Part threethe GPU node

Starved versus merely busy

A training step is a pipeline, and the expensive stage sits downstream of the cheap ones. The GPU being idle is never the cause. PSI on the loader's cgroup tells you which upstream stage lost the time, and full tells you how much.

The boundary of the instrument, stated plainly

PSI localises the resource and the cgroup. It does not identify the code path. It will tell you io.pressure full = 58% on the loader; it will not tell you the stall is in read() on a specific shard over a specific NFS mount.

That is lesson 01b.9's off-CPU flame graph. PSI is the wide-angle instrument; off-CPU analysis is the zoom — and running the zoom first is how people lose the first hour of an incident.

Using itdrills

Drill yourself

Give the one-line difference between some and full.

some = at least one task was stalled. full = every runnable task was stalled, so nothing productive happened at all.

full is the one that converts directly into lost throughput; some tells you there is contention worth understanding.

Why is a process blocked in epoll_wait invisible to PSI?

Because it is idle, not stalled — it does not want a resource it cannot have. PSI counts only tasks that want a resource and are denied it.

That is precisely the distinction load average fails to make: it counts nr_uninterruptible regardless of whether anyone is waiting on the result.

Node CPU is at 90% and the loader's cpu.pressure is near zero. Add cores?

No — do nothing. The node is busy, not starved. Someone is using that CPU productively and your loader is getting what it asks for.

High utilisation with zero pressure is a healthy, well-packed node. Utilisation graphs alone cannot tell you that.

Why alert on total rather than avg10?

avg10/60/300 are decayed EWMAs with fixed time constants — the same structural lag as load average. total is a monotonic microsecond counter, so you can compute an exact rate over whatever window you choose.

In slice 5 all three tasks are in reclaim and the CPU is 100% busy. Is that CPU pressure?

No — it is memory full. NR_RUNNING == NR_MEMSTALL_RUNNING, so every runnable task is stalled on memory reclaim. The CPU is busy, but 100% of that busy-ness is reclaim overhead rather than your work.

A CPU-utilisation graph would show a healthy 100%. That is the failure mode PSI exists to catch.

io.pressure full = 58% on the loader. What does PSI still not tell you?

Which code path. PSI localises the resource (io) and the cgroup (the loader) — not the syscall, the file, or the mount.

For that you need off-CPU analysis from scheduler tracepoints. Reach for PSI first and the flame graph second; the reverse order costs you an hour.

What to keep

  • Waiting, not busyPSI measures time work spent stalled. Utilisation measures time a resource spent occupied. Different questions.
  • some vs fullAt least one stalled, versus everything stalled. Only full is lost throughput.
  • Sleep is invisibleIdle tasks carry no weight, and an all-idle slice carries none at all.
  • Alert on totalMonotonic microseconds. The three averages are EWMAs with the same lag problem as load average.
  • Busy ≠ starvedHigh utilisation with zero pressure is a healthy node. Do not add capacity to it.
  • Pressure on the loaderThe GPU idling is a symptom. PSI on the upstream cgroup names the stage that lost the time.
  • Resource, not code pathPSI is the wide angle. Off-CPU flame graphs are the zoom, and they come second.
