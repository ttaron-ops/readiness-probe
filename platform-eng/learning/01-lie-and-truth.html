Module 05 · 05.1 · GPU observability

100% utilised,
0.34% useful

The number every GPU dashboard shows measures whether a kernel was present, not whether the silicon was working. Here is the arithmetic that proves it, and the two fields that replace it.

Corrects “GPU-Util 100% means saturated” Centrepiece batch-1 decode, derived Hardware H100 SXM5 · 132 SMs · 3.35 TB/s

This is the same shape of problem as load average on a Linux node: a number everyone reads, that answers a question nobody asked, and stays pinned at its maximum while the resource does nothing useful. The difference is that here the number is attached to the most expensive hardware you rent.

Part onethe ambiguity

One word, three questions

A platform team asks a GPU dashboard two completely different questions and uses the same word for both.

Question 1 — occupancy
  • “Is this GPU doing anything at all?”
  • Owner: platform / finance
  • Fix: reclaim the card
  • Answered by SM_ACTIVE
Question 2 — efficiency
  • “Is it doing as much as it can?”
  • Owner: the workload's team
  • Fix: batching, precision, kernels
  • Answered by PIPE_TENSOR_ACTIVE
The default metric answers neither

DCGM_FI_DEV_GPU_UTIL answers a third question nobody asked: “was anything scheduled on this device during the sampling window?”

That third question is cheap to answer, which is why the driver answers it, which is why it is the one number every tool surfaces — nvidia-smi's GPU-Util column, NVIDIA's own DCGM Grafana dashboard, and your cloud provider's GPU graph are all the same counter.

Part twoprovenance

Two paths, different silicon

Before any field semantics, hold this picture. The two families of metric come from different hardware, through different driver interfaces, at different cost, with different failure modes. Almost every confusion in GPU telemetry dissolves once you can draw it.

Part threefield 203

What the lying number actually is

Its collection is a straight passthrough. In DcgmCacheManager.cpp the handler calls nvmlDeviceGetUtilizationRates() once, takes .gpu, casts to long long, and appends it to the cache. No averaging, no scaling, no derivation. Field 203 is nvmlUtilization_t.gpu, unmodified. So the semantics are NVML's semantics:

Say it in one sentence, with no ambiguous word in it

DCGM_FI_DEV_GPU_UTIL is the percentage of a short driver-internal sample window during which at least one kernel was resident on the GPU.

Every clause is load-bearing. “sample window” — 1 s to 1/6 s, so microsecond gaps vanish. “at least one” — one kernel and ten thousand kernels are the same reading. “resident” — not executing usefully, merely present.

Part fourthe derivation

Why batch-1 decode pins it at 100

The general shape of the lie: any workload that keeps a continuous stream of small kernels resident satisfies the predicate ~100% of the time, regardless of how much silicon each kernel uses. Autoregressive LLM decode at batch size 1 is the purest instance — and you can derive the numbers rather than quote them.

Take Llama-3-8B in BF16 on one H100 SXM5.

(b) GPU_UTIL ≈ 100
  • always exactly one kernel resident
  • GEMV → GEMV → attention → next layer
  • gaps are µs of launch latency
  • far below the 1 s sample window
The field is not broken

It is correctly reporting a true fact that is useless. “≥1 kernel resident” is true for essentially the whole window, and it always will be. Measured PIPE_TENSOR_ACTIVE lands a little higher than 0.003 (0.005–0.03) because real kernels are not perfectly bandwidth-efficient and attention adds work — but the order of magnitude is derived, not observed.

Part fivebreadth vs depth

The second arithmetic that matters

SM_ACTIVE is the honest idle signal, but it measures breadth, not intensity. Per SM the hardware keeps elapsed cycles and cycles-with-at-least-one-warp; the ratio is computed per SM and then averaged across every SM on the device.

GPU_UTIL reads 100 in all four quadrants

And in all eight cases once you overlay DRAM_ACTIVE. A single number that is identical across every distinguishable state carries zero diagnostic information.

  Overlay DRAM_ACTIVE (1005) to split A and B further:

     low SM,  low DRAM   → genuinely idle             → reclaim
     low SM,  high DRAM  → memory-bound small-batch   → batch it, don't buy GPUs
     high SM, low DRAM   → stalled on sync/launch     → Nsight Systems
     high SM, high DRAM  → memory-bound at scale      → different GPU / better kernels

The policy that falls out

  1. Page on SM_ACTIVE, gated by FB_USED

    The reclaim alert is “allocated, and doing no work for long enough that this is not a gap between steps.” Not a gap — a vacancy.

  2. Warn, never page, on PIPE_TENSOR_ACTIVE

    Efficiency is a conversation, not an incident. It belongs in a weekly report and a dashboard, not in PagerDuty at 03:00.

  3. Never alert on GPU_UTIL at all

    There is no threshold on a constant that means anything.

Using itdrills

Drill yourself

A dashboard shows GPU_UTIL = 100% across 64 GPUs. What have you learned?

That at least one kernel was resident on each device during each sample window. Nothing about how much silicon was used, how many SMs were lit, or whether any math happened.

It is consistent with a fully saturated training job and with 64 cards running batch-1 decode at 0.3% tensor utilisation.

Derive why batch-1 decode of an 8B model pins the counter at 100.

16 GB of weights must cross HBM per token with no reuse: 16 GB ÷ 3.35 TB/s ≈ 4.78 ms. The maths is 2 × 8e9 = 16 GFLOP ÷ 989 TFLOP/s ≈ 16.2 µs. So compute is 0.34% of the step.

But throughout those 4.78 ms there is always exactly one kernel resident, and the inter-kernel gaps are microseconds — far below NVML's 1 s to 1/6 s window. The predicate is true the whole time.

A kernel keeps 32 of an H100's 132 SMs 100% busy. What does SM_ACTIVE read, and why?

0.24. The ratio is computed per SM and averaged over all 132, so 32/132 ≈ 0.242 — even though every one of those 32 SMs is fully occupied.

SM_ACTIVE is a breadth measure. Low SM_ACTIVE means "not much of the GPU is involved," not "the GPU is doing nothing."

Why gate the reclaim alert on FB_USED?

Because an inference server holding a loaded model and a warm KV cache can legitimately sit at near-zero SM_ACTIVE between requests. Reclaiming it would kill a live service.

FB_USED < 2048 distinguishes "nothing is here" from "something is here and idle."

High SM_ACTIVE, near-zero PIPE_TENSOR_ACTIVE. Diagnosis?

Quadrant A — the SMs are lit but not doing tensor math. Spin-waits, __syncthreads() storms, NCCL collective waits, or FP32 kernels where BF16 would do.

This is not a reclaim case: the GPU is allocated and busy. It is an efficiency case, and it escalates to the profiling ladder.

Why can't you derive one path's metrics from the other?

Path A integrates a single scheduler bit and has no notion of how many SMs exist. Path B reads per-SM hardware counters and has no notion of whether a context was bound.

They measure different physical things through different driver interfaces. Arriving on the same /metrics endpoint with the same labels is a packaging artefact, not shared provenance — and it is exactly why people assume otherwise.
