450 down to 50,

The ratio the whole module rolls downhill from — plus the reason the NIC binds by only 8%, and what that means when a PCIe link quietly trains down.

The cliff 9 : 1, per GPU per direction Binding hop min(450, 54, 50) Scale cost 64 → 4,096 GPUs = +14%

One number governs this whole module: a gradient byte leaving a GPU drops from 450 GB/s to 50 GB/s the moment it needs another node. Nine to one. Every design decision in modules 08 and 09 rolls downhill from that gradient.

Part onethe cliff

The ladder, per GPU, per direction

Units discipline, first — this is where bad answers come from

NVLink and PCIe are quoted in GB/s (bytes). NICs and switches in Gb/s (bits). Divide by 8 to compare: 400 Gb/s ÷ 8 = 50 GB/s.

(a) NVLink 4 450 GB/s
(b) PCIe Gen5 x16 63 GB/s
    …usable ~54 GB/s
(c) ConnectX-7 NDR 400G 50 GB/s

Each hop is a ceiling; the transfer runs at min(a, b, c) = 50 GB/s. Ratio (a) : (c) = 9 : 1.

  1. The NIC binds — but only just

    min(450, 54, 50) = 50. The PCIe link sits only 8% above the NIC. A link that has quietly trained down moves the bottleneck from the wire to the bus without a single fabric setting changing — and a Gen5 x8 link (31.5 GB/s) caps a 400G NIC at 63% of line rate, with nothing in the NIC's own counters explaining why.

  2. Next generation, the constraint moves

    ConnectX-8 is 800 Gb/s = 100 GB/s per direction, which a PCIe Gen5 x16 host link cannot feed — 63 GB/s is 37% below line rate. That is exactly why ConnectX-8 is a native PCIe Gen6 x16 device: the NIC generation and the PCIe generation had to move together.

    A build sheet pairing an 800G NIC with a Gen5 host link is a real finding: you paid for 800G optics to get about 500G.

  3. The cliff is why the rest of module 09 exists

    Rail-optimised cabling exists to spend your 50 GB/s on one switch hop instead of three. Oversubscription exists because cliff-crossing traffic is a small, predictable fraction. SHARP exists to reduce how many bytes cross at all.

Part twothe mechanism

What actually happens at each hop

#StepWhere it can go wrong
1NCCL decides. It has built a topology graph of the node — the same PCIe and NVLink topology topo -m renders — and chosen per channel which GPU sends to which peer over which transport.A misread topology picks a worse transport silently.
2A work request is posted. The process writes a work-queue entry and rings a doorbell — a write to a memory-mapped NIC register. No syscall.—
3The NIC DMAs the payload. With GPUDirect RDMA the source addresses are GPU HBM exposed through a BAR, so reads are peer-to-peer under one PCIe switch.Without it — different root complexes — the driver stages through pinned host DRAM: doubling PCIe traffic and adding latency and jitter.

5Bits hit the cable. 400G NDR is 4 lanes at 100 Gb/s effective. The definitional start of “the fabric”.—
Part threethe cost

What the cliff costs a job spanning M nodes

A hierarchical rail-aware all-reduce — what NCCL actually runs on a multi-node HGX cluster — in three phases. M nodes × 8 GPUs, each holding S bytes of gradient.

Using itdrills

Drill yourself

How much slower is inter-node than intra-node, and which hop binds?

9:1 — 450 GB/s NVLink against 50 GB/s for a 400G NIC, both per direction. The NIC binds, at min(450, 54, 50).

But only by 8%: usable PCIe Gen5 x16 is ~54 GB/s, so a degraded link moves the bottleneck to the bus.

A build sheet pairs an 800G ConnectX-8 with a PCIe Gen5 x16 host link. Problem?

Yes, a real one. ConnectX-8 puts 100 GB/s per direction on the wire; Gen5 x16 tops out at 63 GB/s theoretical — 37% below line rate.

ConnectX-8 is specified as a native PCIe Gen6 x16 device precisely because the two generations had to move together. You would be paying for 800G optics to get about 500G.

GPU and NIC are on different root complexes. What does that cost?

GPUDirect RDMA stops applying, so the driver stages through pinned host DRAM: GPU → DRAM at PCIe speed, then DRAM → NIC. That doubles PCIe traffic and adds latency and jitter.

topo -m shows this as SYS rather than PIX.

Does an all-reduce get much slower going from 64 to 4,096 GPUs?

No — about 14%. The NIC term is 2(M−1)/M · (S/8) / B_nic, and 2(M−1)/M converges to 2: 1.750 at M=8 versus 1.996 at M=512.

A ring all-reduce is asymptotically size-independent. What grows at scale is the chance that some link is degraded, not the arithmetic.

Why does 400 Gb/s become 50 GB/s?

Bits versus bytes: ÷8. NICs and switches are quoted in Gb/s, NVLink and PCIe in GB/s. Measured ceiling lands nearer 46–48 GB/s after protocol overhead.

Getting this wrong in either direction is the single most common arithmetic error in fabric discussions.
