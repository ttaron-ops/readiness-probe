Module 07 · 07.2 · Inference serving

Concurrency is
a division
problem

Free VRAM after weights, divided by per-request KV bytes. Every serving decision you make is an attempt to change one of those two numbers.

Formula 2 × L × n_kv × d × e_kv Worked Llama-3.1-8B on one H100-80GB Derived 79.7% internal fragmentation

Strip the transformer away and the serving problem is an allocator problem — one with a nastier profile than anything in a normal heap. Once you see it that way, the concurrency cap is arithmetic and the 60–80% waste figure is something you derive rather than cite.

Part onethe reframe

It is a heap allocator, with the hard properties

PropertyWhy it hurts
Fixed-size pool, sized once at process startNo growth path. You cannot ask the OS for more.
Allocations arrive unpredictablyEach one is a sequence; you cannot batch admissions.
Each grows monotonically, one token per decode stepNever shrinks until it dies.
Final size unknown at allocation timeThe model decides when to emit EOS. You cannot ask.
Freed all at once when the request finishesFreeing is the only way capacity returns.
Deliberately oversubscribedIdle pool space is idle GPU, so you admit as many as you dare.
Capacity failure
  • live allocations exceed the pool
  • no allocator design saves you
  • something is evicted or refused
  • this is physics → §2–3
Efficiency failure
  • useful bytes far below pool size
  • but you still cannot admit one more
  • the bytes are in an unusable shape
  • this is a design choice → §4

Three readings, and the middle one surprises people

The MHA row is why GQA carries the economics. Same parameter count, same quality class, 8× the KV — so 8× fewer seats, and since decode throughput is roughly linear in resident batch, roughly 8× the cost per million tokens at long context. num_key_value_heads is a first-class cost input when evaluating a model, not a modelling detail.

405B is not 16× the 8B — it is 3.9×. Because n_kv stayed at 8 while L went 32 → 126. Per-token KV scales with depth, not parameter count. A wide shallow model is cheap to cache; a deep narrow one is not. This is why you cannot estimate KV from "model size."

The dtype term is a free 2×. --kv-cache-dtype fp8 halves every number above, independently of weight precision. bf16 weights with an fp8 cache is perfectly ordinary. The cost is an accuracy delta you measure, not assume.

Three architectures break the formula — all downward

Sliding-window attention caches at most W tokens per windowed layer, so its contribution saturates. Cross-layer KV sharing makes the effective L equal L / group_size. MLA (DeepSeek-V2/V3) compresses K and V into one low-rank latent per token per layer.

vLLM models these as separate KV-cache groups with their own specs and sums per-group block requirements. If your model is hybrid, trust the printed startup line over your formula.

(b) expected

The cap that binds is min(a-or-b, c)

And which one binds is the most important diagnostic fact about your deployment, because the fixes are completely different. Memory-bound → shrink max_model_len, fp8 the cache, raise gpu-memory-utilization. Scheduler-bound → raise max_num_seqs, and no amount of memory work will help.

Part fourthe waste

Deriving the 60–80%, not quoting it

Do the obvious thing — the thing every pre-2023 stack did, and a hand-rolled generate() loop still does: give each sequence one contiguous KV buffer, sized to the maximum it could need, at admission time.

You have to size it to the maximum, and that is not laziness. Three constraints force it:

  1. The final length is unknown

    The model decides when to stop. There is nothing to ask.

  2. A contiguous buffer cannot grow in place

    Same reason realloc sometimes copies — and copying a multi-gigabyte KV tensor mid-decode is not an option.

  3. The attention kernel wants a contiguous stride

    A naive kernel indexes K[layer][head][position] with a fixed stride. Non-contiguity requires gathering through an indirection — a kernel change, not a config change.

The derivation above is steady-state. Add reservation-for-growth — a request that will eventually use all 2,048 tokens still holds all of it from step 1 — and the time-averaged figure is worse. Add external fragmentation holes between freed buffers and it drops again.

Using itdrills

Drill yourself

Why is Llama-3.1-405B's KV only 3.9× the 8B's, not 16×?

Because n_kv stayed at 8 in both, while L went from 32 to 126. Per-token KV is 2 × L × n_kv × d × e_kv — it scales with depth, not parameter count.

Consequence: you cannot estimate KV from "model size." Read config.json.

Two 70B models, same quality. One uses GQA with n_kv=8, the other MHA with n_kv=64. Cost difference?

8× the KV per token → 8× fewer concurrent seats. Since decode throughput is roughly linear in resident batch size well below the ridge point, that is roughly 8× the cost per million tokens at long context.

Which is why num_key_value_heads is a procurement input, not a modelling footnote.

vLLM prints "Maximum concurrency: 48.8x". You observe 120 concurrent requests. Bug?

No. The printed figure is cap (a) — worst case, assuming every request runs to the full max_model_len. Real sequences hold blocks proportional to their current length, which time-averages to about P + M/2.

It is a floor on what you can serve, not a prediction of what you will.

You double the KV pool and concurrency does not move. What is happening?

You are bound by cap (c) — max_num_seqs, the scheduler's hard limit on sequences per forward pass. It is independent of memory, so no amount of pool work will change anything.

Knowing which of the three caps binds is the most important diagnostic fact about a deployment.

Derive the internal fragmentation of a contiguous allocator on chat traffic.

Weight each output-length bucket by its share and compare expected used tokens against the reservation. For a ShareGPT-shaped distribution at max_model_len = 2048: 416 expected tokens used out of 2,048 reserved = 20.3% utilisation, so ~80% internal fragmentation.

And that is before reservation-for-growth and external fragmentation.

Why must a contiguous allocator reserve the maximum?

Final length is unknown (the model decides when to stop); a contiguous buffer cannot grow in place if a neighbour is occupied, and copying multi-gigabyte KV mid-decode is not viable; and the naive attention kernel indexes with a fixed stride, so non-contiguity needs a kernel change.

All three point at the same fix, which is the next lesson.

What to keep

  • An allocator problemFixed pool, unpredictable arrivals, monotonic growth, unknown final size, free-all-at-once.
  • 2·L·n_kv·d·e_kvPer-token KV. Depth and KV-head count dominate; parameter count does not appear.
  • n_kv is a cost inputGQA vs MHA at the same quality is an 8× concurrency difference.
  • KV is the residualPool = utilisation × VRAM − weights − working set. That is why the utilisation flag matters so much.
  • Three capsWorst-case, expected, and max_num_seqs. Which one binds decides the fix entirely.
  • P + M/2Time-averaged residency, not P + M. This is why observed concurrency beats the printed floor.
  • 80% waste, derived416 used of 2,048 reserved on chat-shaped traffic. Own the derivation, not the citation.
  • Efficiency is a choiceCapacity failure is physics. Fragmentation is a design decision — and it is the one PagedAttention reverses.
